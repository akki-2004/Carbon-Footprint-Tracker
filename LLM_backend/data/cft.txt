Q1: What is a carbon footprint?
A1: A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases generated by our actions, expressed in carbon dioxide equivalents. It includes emissions from transportation, electricity use, food production, and manufacturing. Reducing it is vital to mitigate climate change.

Q2: Why is carbon neutrality important?
A2: Carbon neutrality is crucial because it helps fight climate change by reducing the net emissions of greenhouse gases. It limits global temperature rise, helps protect ecosystems, and ensures a sustainable future by minimizing the harmful effects of carbon pollution on the environment and public health.

Q3: What is carbon neutrality?
A3: Carbon neutrality refers to achieving a balance between the carbon dioxide emitted into the atmosphere and the carbon removed or offset. This can be accomplished by reducing emissions through renewable energy, efficiency improvements, and offsetting the remaining emissions through environmental projects like reforestation.

Q4: How can a country become carbon neutral?
A4: A country can become carbon neutral by drastically cutting greenhouse gas emissions, transitioning to renewable energy, implementing strict environmental policies, promoting green technologies, and offsetting the remaining emissions through carbon credits or sustainable projects such as afforestation and renewable infrastructure investments.
